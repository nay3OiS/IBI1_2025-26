This practical involved implementing three different versions of the SIR model in Python, to explore how infectious diseases spread in populations. I created a basic random SIR simulation, a vaccination SIR model to study herd immunity, and a spatial SIR model to visualise transmission across a 2D grid.
Working with the probabilistic logic was initially challenging, especially calculating infection probabilities and using np.random.choice to simulate random events. Updating the counts of susceptible, infected, and recovered individuals without introducing errors also required careful attention. The spatial model was particularly complex, as it required managing a 2D grid, implementing neighbour-based infection rules, and setting up dynamic visualisation. Despite these difficulties, the process helped me see how simple computational models can capture key real-world behaviours. I observed how different vaccination rates change the spread of infection, and how spatial structure can slow or accelerate an outbreak.
Overall, this practical improved my confidence with loops, NumPy arrays, and Matplotlib visualisation. It also gave me a clearer understanding of how computational modelling is used in epidemiology to test interventions and predict outcomes.
